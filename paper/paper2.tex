\title{A Hierarchy Needs a Landscape:\\
Recovering Renormalization Hierarchies in Enumerable Dynamical Systems}
\author{Zaccarie Aglas\\ Independent Researcher\\ \texttt{zaccarie@zaccarie.fr}}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
A coarse-graining hierarchy --- a tower of successively coarser descriptions,
each consistent with the dynamics --- is the object renormalization,
multi-scale community detection, and computational mechanics all reach for.
When does such a hierarchy exist for a given dynamical system, and can it be
recovered without supervision? We study the question on discrete dynamical
systems small enough to enumerate completely, so that the transition operator,
its spectrum, the stationary measure, and every mutual information are computed
\emph{exactly} --- no sampling, no variational estimator. We report four
things. (i)~Agglomerative Information Bottleneck, run with exact mutual
information, recovers the renormalization quotient; on a deterministic
automaton it recovers the basin partition exactly, as a zero-cost merge
plateau. (ii)~On a Markov chain with a planted nested-metastable structure, the
transfer-operator spectrum and the agglomerative bottleneck recover every
planted level, and agree. (iii)~The hierarchy is a \emph{Markov-time flow}: as
the random-walk time grows, fine levels dissolve in order and coarse ones
persist --- time is the renormalization parameter. (iv)~The hierarchy does
\emph{not} emerge from a noised deterministic automaton --- a near-permutation
operator with no spectral gap --- but \emph{does} emerge, ex nihilo, from a
spin glass: a rough random landscape grows a multi-level metastable hierarchy
as it cools. The condition is landscape roughness, not stochasticity. A
hierarchy needs a landscape.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Many ideas about structure across scales share one object: a \emph{hierarchy of
coarse-grainings}. The renormalization group builds a sequence of effective
descriptions, each obtained by integrating out fine detail. Multi-scale
community detection reads a network at a ladder of resolutions. Computational
mechanics compresses a process to its causal states, the minimal sufficient
statistic of its future~\citep{crutchfield1989}. In each case the object is a tower
$Q_0 \supset Q_1 \supset \cdots$ of partitions of the state space, each coarser
than the last and each still compatible with the dynamics.

Two questions are usually entangled and rarely answered exactly. \emph{When
does such a hierarchy exist} for a given dynamical system? And \emph{can it be
recovered} from the dynamics alone, without being told the levels in advance?
The difficulty is methodological: on a large model the hierarchy is never known
exactly, so a recovered tower cannot be scored against ground truth, and
mutual-information quantities must be estimated, with all the bias that entails.

We remove the methodological gap by working on dynamical systems small enough
to enumerate completely. The full state space, the transition operator, its
spectrum, the stationary measure, and every joint distribution --- hence every
mutual information --- are computed \emph{exactly}, without sampling. On such a
system a recovered hierarchy can be compared to an exact reference, and the
Information Bottleneck can be run with the true joint $p(x,y)$ rather than an
estimate. (The same enumerable testbed underlies a companion study of
representation learning; here we study its multi-scale structure.)

We report four results.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The method recovers the quotient}
    (Section~\ref{sec:method}). Agglomerative Information Bottleneck, run with
    exact mutual information, recovers the renormalization quotient. On a
    deterministic automaton, with the dynamical fate as the relevant variable,
    it recovers the basin partition \emph{exactly}, as a zero-cost merge
    plateau --- but the deterministic hierarchy is only one level deep.
  \item \textbf{A planted hierarchy is recovered exactly}
    (Section~\ref{sec:planted}). On a Markov chain with a planted
    nested-metastable structure, the transfer-operator spectrum and the
    agglomerative bottleneck each recover every planted level, and agree.
  \item \textbf{The hierarchy is a Markov-time flow}
    (Section~\ref{sec:flow}). On a random walk over a modular graph, sweeping
    the walk time makes the recovered hierarchy coarsen: fine levels dissolve
    in order, coarse ones persist. Markov time is the renormalization
    parameter.
  \item \textbf{A hierarchy needs a landscape}
    (Section~\ref{sec:emergence}). No hierarchy emerges from a noised
    deterministic automaton --- its transfer operator is a near-permutation
    with no spectral gap. A deep hierarchy \emph{does} emerge, with nothing
    planted, from a spin glass: a rough random energy landscape grows a
    multi-level metastable tower as it cools. The condition is landscape
    roughness, not stochasticity.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Bisimulation and state abstraction.}
Bisimulation metrics quantify the behavioural equivalence of states
\citep{ferns2004metrics}; learned variants --- deep bisimulation for control
\citep{zhang2021dbc} and MICo \citep{castro2021mico} --- train representations
that collapse behaviourally equivalent states, and are judged by downstream
control return. The bisimulation quotient is precisely the object these methods
approximate. We differ in where we stand: on an enumerable system the quotient
is \emph{known exactly}, so a learned objective can be \emph{audited} against
ground truth rather than validated only by reward. The recursive objective of
Section~\ref{sec:method} is the bisimulation fixed-point equation, and the
testbed scores how well it recovers the exact quotient.

\paragraph{World models and learned latents.}
Discrete-latent and tokenized world models \citep{vandenoord2017vqvae,
hafner2023dreamerv3} learn a latent dynamics from observations. Whether the
latent captures the \emph{causal-dynamical} structure of the environment ---
which states are dynamically equivalent, how trajectories converge --- is
normally unknowable: there is no ground-truth dynamical partition to check
against. The present testbed supplies one.

\paragraph{Computational mechanics.}
The causal states of a process \citep{crutchfield1989} are the minimal
sufficient statistic of its future --- the coarsest partition of histories that
preserves prediction. The renormalization quotient is the
deterministic-dynamics counterpart, and the agglomerative bottleneck of
Section~\ref{sec:method} recovers it. We add exact enumeration: the causal
partition is computed, not estimated.

\paragraph{Multi-scale community detection.}
Reading a network at a ladder of random-walk timescales --- Markov stability
\citep{delvenne2010stability} --- is the closest precedent for the Markov-time
flow of Section~\ref{sec:flow}. We recast it as a renormalization flow on a
dynamical system, and run it against exact ground truth.

\paragraph{Position.}
Across these literatures the gap is the same: the dynamical structure a
representation ought to capture is never known exactly, so methods are learned
and validated only indirectly. Our contribution is orthogonal --- an
\emph{exact-ground-truth audit}. On enumerable dynamical systems the
bisimulation quotient, the transfer spectrum, and every mutual information are
computed without sampling; a representation learner is then measured against the
true answer.

\section{Setup: Enumerable Dynamics and the Renormalization Quotient}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Systems.}
We study dynamical systems on a finite state space $S$ small enough to
enumerate. A \emph{deterministic} system is a map $\succf : S \to S$ --- for
us, an elementary cellular automaton (ECA) on $n$ cells, $|S| = 2^n$~\citep{wuensche1999z}. A
\emph{stochastic} system is a row-stochastic transition matrix $P$ on $S$ ---
a Markov chain. In both cases, because $|S|$ is enumerated, we hold the
\emph{entire} operator: the functional graph $\succf$, or the matrix $P$ with
its powers $P^t$, its eigendecomposition, and its stationary measure $\pi$. No
quantity below is sampled.

\paragraph{The renormalization quotient.}
Fix a coarse base observable $\ell : S \to L$. The \emph{renormalization
quotient} relative to $\ell$ is the coarsest partition of $S$ that refines
$\ell$ and is compatible with the dynamics --- $x \equiv x'$ implies their
successors are equivalent. It is the bisimulation quotient under the dynamics;
it is computed exactly by partition refinement \citep{paigetarjan1987},
converging in at most $|S|$ steps. Without a base observable coarser than the
identity the quotient of a deterministic system is trivial: $\ell$ is what
defines the macroscopic variables. A \emph{hierarchy} is a tower of such
quotients at increasing coarseness.

\paragraph{The transfer operator.}
For a Markov chain, $P$ is the transfer (Koopman) operator. Its eigenvalues
near $1$ are the slow modes --- the metastable coordinates; gaps in the
spectrum of relaxation times $t = -1/\log|\lambda|$ mark the boundaries between
scales. For a deterministic system $\succf$ the transfer operator is a
permutation matrix, a fact that will matter in Section~\ref{sec:emergence}.

\section{Method: Agglomerative Bottleneck with Exact Mutual Information}
\label{sec:method}

To recover a hierarchy \emph{without} being given the levels, we need an
objective that builds the tower bottom-up. We use the agglomerative
Information Bottleneck \citep{slonim2000aib,tishby1999ib}.

Fix a \emph{relevant variable} $Y$ --- the dynamical future of a state (the
fate it reaches, or its distribution after $t$ steps). Each state begins in its
own block. At each step we merge the pair of blocks whose merge loses the least
relevant information $I(Z;Y)$; the Slonim--Tishby merge cost is
\[
  \Delta I(z_i,z_j) \;=\; \big(p(z_i)+p(z_j)\big)\;
  \mathrm{JS}\big(p(y\mid z_i),\,p(y\mid z_j)\big),
\]
the Jensen--Shannon divergence of the two future distributions, weighted by
mass. The sequence of merges is a dendrogram --- the hierarchy
$Q_0 \supset Q_1 \supset \cdots$, with no level imposed in advance.

The enumerable setting is what makes this exact. Because the state space is
enumerated and the dynamics is known, the joint $p(x,y)$ is exact; hence
$I(Z;Y)$, and every merge cost, is computed \emph{exactly}. There is no
variational lower bound and no estimator bias --- the usual obstacle to
information-theoretic clustering is simply absent.

\paragraph{Validation on a deterministic automaton.}
On an ECA, take the relevant variable to be the dynamical fate --- the
attractor each state reaches. States in the same basin have identical fate, so
their merge cost is exactly zero; the agglomerative bottleneck performs all
within-basin merges first, at zero cost, and the partition at that plateau is
exactly the basin partition (adjusted mutual information $1.0$ with the true
basins, on ordered, complex, and chaotic rules alike). The macroscopic variable
``which basin'' is not imposed --- it is \emph{discovered}, as the coarsest
lossless compression of the state space relative to fate.

\paragraph{The relevant variable gates the hierarchy.}
The recovered hierarchy depends entirely on $Y$. With the dynamical fate as
$Y$, the basin partition emerges; with a perceptual observable --- the cell
density of the far-future state --- a \emph{different}, finer partition emerges,
one whose adjusted mutual information with the basins is near zero. What
emerges is the structure relevant to the chosen $Y$, nothing more. This is a
useful warning, but on a deterministic system it is also the end of the story:
the fate is a one-shot label, so the recovered hierarchy is a single level ---
states, then basins, then nothing. A deep tower needs a richer substrate. The
rest of the paper supplies one.

\section{Exact Recovery of a Planted Hierarchy}
\label{sec:planted}

We first check that a genuine multi-level hierarchy, when one exists, is
recovered. We plant one. A \emph{hierarchical Markov chain} is built from a
tree of blocks: $b$ children per node, $D$ levels, $m$ micro-states per leaf;
two states communicate at a rate that decays with the depth of their lowest
common ancestor --- fast mixing within a leaf, slow between deep branches. The
planted tree is the ground truth. We test a three-level chain
($2^3$ leaves $\times\, 8$ micro-states $= 64$ states).

Two independent readings recover it (Table~\ref{tab:planted}). The transfer
spectrum sorts the relaxation times into clean packets; the number of modes per
packet is exactly $\#\text{blocks}(d) - \#\text{blocks}(d{+}1)$, so the spectrum
\emph{encodes} the planted tree. The agglomerative bottleneck, with the
one-step future as $Y$, recovers every planted level at adjusted mutual
information $1.0$. Spectrum and bottleneck --- one linear-algebraic, one
information-theoretic --- agree exactly.

\section{The Hierarchy Is a Markov-Time Flow}
\label{sec:flow}

A hierarchy is not a static partition but a \emph{flow}. We make the point on a
random walk over a modular graph: a graph with nested communities (edge
probability decaying with the depth of the lowest common ancestor), sampled,
with the walk $P$ the row-normalized adjacency. The community topology is
planted; the metastable structure \emph{emerges} from the walk dynamics.

We then sweep the Markov time $t$ and read the partition of $P^t$ with the
agglomerative bottleneck. As $t$ grows, the recovered hierarchy
\emph{coarsens} (Table~\ref{tab:flow}): the finest level dissolves first ---
intra-community mixing has erased it --- then the next, while the coarsest
survives. Each level owns a window of Markov time. The tower
$Q(t{=}1) \supset Q(t{=}8) \supset Q(t{=}32)$ is a renormalization flow with
\emph{time} as the flow parameter, the dynamical counterpart of the multi-scale
community structure read off random-walk timescales in Markov-stability
analysis \citep{delvenne2010stability}.

\section{When a Hierarchy Emerges}
\label{sec:emergence}

Sections~\ref{sec:planted}--\ref{sec:flow} planted the structure --- the rates,
or the topology --- and recovered it. The harder question is when a hierarchy
emerges with \emph{nothing} planted. We contrast two cases.

\paragraph{No hierarchy: a noised automaton.}
Take an ECA and make it stochastic: with probability $1-\epsilon$ apply the
rule, with probability $\epsilon$ step to a perceptual neighbour (a one-bit
flip). One might hope the deterministic basins become metastable sets. They do
not, in any usable sense. The transfer operator of a deterministic automaton is
a \emph{permutation}: its entire spectrum lies on the unit circle. Adding
perceptual noise only perturbs that spectrum slightly --- most eigenvalue moduli
stay near $1$, and \emph{no spectral gap} opens to separate slow modes from
fast (Table~\ref{tab:noise}, $45$ to $179$ eigenvalues of modulus above $0.5$
out of $256$). Only the escape time scales cleanly, as $1/\epsilon$. A
near-permutation plus noise is not a metastable mixer; it has no hierarchy to
find.

\paragraph{A hierarchy, ex nihilo: a spin glass.}
Now take a system with a genuine \emph{landscape}. An Ising spin
glass~\citep{mezard1987} on $n$ spins has energy
$E(s) = -\sum_{i<j} J_{ij}\,s_i s_j$ with i.i.d.\ Gaussian
couplings $J_{ij}$; single-spin-flip Glauber dynamics gives a Markov chain
whose stationary measure is exactly Boltzmann. Nothing about a hierarchy is
planted --- the couplings are random. Yet as the temperature falls, a
multi-level metastable hierarchy \emph{emerges} (Table~\ref{tab:glass}): the
number of well-separated relaxation-time gaps grows $1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4$, and
the slow timescales spread over eleven orders of magnitude. The slow modes
appear in degenerate \emph{pairs}, the signature of the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry
$E(-s) = E(s)$ --- metastable states come in $\pm$ pairs. The rough random
landscape, on its own, grows a renormalization tower.

\paragraph{The condition.}
Both systems are stochastic; only one grows a hierarchy. The difference is not
stochasticity but \emph{landscape}. A noised automaton has no energy landscape
--- a permutation plus noise --- and no metastable tower forms. A spin glass has
a rugged landscape of nested minima, and a tower forms spontaneously. For a
renormalization hierarchy to emerge, the dynamics must sit on a rough landscape
with nested basins. A hierarchy needs a landscape.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the testbed buys.}
Every quantity here --- the transfer spectrum, the stationary measure, the
mutual informations driving the agglomerative bottleneck --- is exact, because
the state space is enumerated. The recovered hierarchies are scored against
exact references (planted trees, or the exact basin partition). This is the
methodological point: information-theoretic hierarchy recovery, usually
hostage to estimator bias and to the absence of ground truth, becomes a
controlled measurement.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The systems are small ($|S| \le 4096$) by design --- exactness requires
enumeration. The recovery experiments of
Sections~\ref{sec:planted}--\ref{sec:flow} use planted or semi-planted
substrates: this is a controlled-recovery design, and what is validated is that
the method recovers a hierarchy \emph{known} to be present, not that a
hierarchy arises unprompted. Only Section~\ref{sec:emergence} is unplanted, and
there the spin-glass result at $n{=}10$ is modest --- a four-gap tower; the
ultrametric organization of spin-glass states is a large-$n$ phenomenon and is
only incipient here. The agglomerative bottleneck costs $O(|S|^2)$ in memory
and time, which is the practical ceiling on $|S|$.

\paragraph{Structural resonance with discrete-spacetime programs.}
The obstruction that motivates this work --- discretizing a continuous system
without losing causal structure --- is the same obstruction addressed by several
programs in quantum gravity, and the mathematical responses share a common
skeleton.  In loop quantum gravity, spatial areas are discrete not because a
grid is imposed, but because the \emph{spectrum} of the area operator is
discrete~\cite{Rovelli1995}; the spectrum encodes levels, not coordinates.
Here, the levels of the renormalization hierarchy are exactly the gaps in the
spectrum of the transfer operator (Section~\ref{sec:flow}) --- discreteness
from eigenvalues, not from quantization of the state.
In the causal-set program, spacetime is a partially ordered set, and
``order plus counting equals geometry'' (Sorkin): the geometry is not
a background but an \emph{emergent} property of the relational structure.
The transition graph $f$ is a partial order; the stationary measure~$\mu$
is counting; the hierarchy that emerges is geometry.
In multiscale entanglement renormalization (MERA)~\cite{Vidal2007}, a
tensor-network hierarchy is interpreted as a holographic bulk: the
renormalization-group direction becomes an emergent spatial dimension.
The Markov-time flow of Section~\ref{sec:flow} is the same structure in
miniature: Markov time $t$ is the renormalization parameter, and the hierarchy
is the emergent ``bulk''.
These are analogies, not derivations.  But they share a single lesson:
the unit of discretization should be the \emph{invariant} --- causal order,
gauge holonomy, renormalization equivalence class --- rather than a coordinate.
The agglomerative bottleneck operates on mutual information, an invariant of
the dynamics; that is why it recovers the hierarchy where naive grid
quantization collapses it.

\paragraph{Outlook.}
The natural next step is to push the unplanted case --- larger spin glasses, or
other rough-landscape dynamics --- to ask not only whether a hierarchy emerges
but whether it is ultrametric, and to follow the Markov-time flow of an
\emph{emergent} (rather than planted) tower.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On discrete dynamical systems with exact ground truth, a renormalization
hierarchy --- the tower of dynamics-compatible coarse-grainings --- can be
recovered without supervision: the agglomerative Information Bottleneck, run
with exact mutual information, and the transfer-operator spectrum recover a
planted hierarchy exactly and agree. The hierarchy is not a static object but a
flow: Markov time is its renormalization parameter, and the levels dissolve in
order as time grows. And a hierarchy is not free --- it does not emerge from a
noised deterministic automaton, whose transfer operator is a near-permutation
with no spectral gap, but it does emerge, with nothing planted, from a spin
glass. The dividing line is the landscape: a renormalization hierarchy needs a
rough landscape of nested basins. A hierarchy needs a landscape.
