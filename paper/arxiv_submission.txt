ABSTRACT  (paste as plain text into the arXiv abstract field)
------------------------------------------------------------
Representation learning is usually evaluated without access to the
causal structure of the data: a probe reaches 85% accuracy and we
cannot tell whether a feature is genuinely represented or the number
is an artifact. We introduce a testbed in which the causal structure
is known exactly. On elementary cellular automata and random Boolean
networks small enough to enumerate, the entire dynamics -- basins of
attraction, attractors, garden-of-Eden states, the bisimulation
quotient -- is computed exactly and used as ground truth. With it we
show four things. (i) A model trained only to predict the forward
dynamics develops a representation that linearly encodes local causal
facts (garden-of-Eden membership, in-degree). (ii) A compressed,
structure-preserving discretization of the state space provably
exists -- the dynamical quotient. (iii) Yet five standard
learned-tokenizer objectives -- reconstruction, next-step prediction,
multi-horizon prediction, and two temporal-contrastive variants --
all fail to recover it. We explain the failure: the dynamical
quotient is perceptually shattered, as dispersed in observation space
as a random partition, so objectives biased toward observation
proximity optimize a geometry orthogonal to the causal one. The
barrier is structural, not an optimization artifact. (iv) The failure
is nonetheless specific to the objective class: an objective defined
recursively on the dynamics -- a learned bisimulation metric --
recovers the quotient (adjusted mutual information up to 0.95), where
every observation-proximity objective stays near zero. A single
inductive bias -- locality -- unifies the local success and the
global failure.
------------------------------------------------------------
